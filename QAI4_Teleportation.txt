## 2. CONCEPTS USED IN THE PRACTICAL

### 2.1 Quantum Teleportation
Quantum teleportation is a process of transferring an unknown quantum state from a sender's qubit to a receiver's qubit using:
- Entanglement between two qubits.
- Measurement of the sender's qubits.
- Classical communication of measurement results.
- Conditional quantum gates applied by the receiver.

It transfers the **quantum state**, not the physical qubit itself.

### 2.2 Qubits
A qubit is the basic unit of quantum information. Unlike a classical bit, which is either 0 or 1, a qubit can exist in a superposition of both states.

### 2.3 Quantum Entanglement
Entanglement is a quantum phenomenon in which two or more qubits share a correlated state. Measuring one qubit affects the possible measurement outcomes of the other.

### 2.4 Quantum Gates Used

| Gate | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `RY` | Rotates a qubit around the Y-axis. |
| `RZ` | Rotates a qubit around the Z-axis. |
| `H` | Creates superposition or changes the measurement basis. |
| `CX` | Flips the target qubit when the control qubit is 1. |
| `X` | Bit-flip gate; changes 0 to 1 and 1 to 0. |
| `Z` | Phase-flip gate; changes the phase of the \(|1\rangle\) component. |
| `Barrier` | Separates stages of the quantum circuit. |
| `Measure` | Converts quantum information into classical measurement results. |

### C. Preparing the Initial Quantum State

**Line 4: `qc.ry(1.0, 0)`**

- Applies an `RY` rotation gate to qubit 0.
- The angle of rotation is 1.0 radian.
- It rotates the qubit around the Y-axis of the Bloch sphere.
- Starting from \(|0\rangle\), it creates a superposition:

\[
|\psi\rangle = \cos(0.5)|0\rangle+\sin(0.5)|1\rangle

This is the initial quantum state that we want to teleport.

**Possible questions:**
- **Q: What does the RY gate do?**  
  A: It rotates a qubit around the Y-axis.

  A: To prepare a quantum state containing both \(|0\rangle\) and \(|1\rangle\) components.

**Line 5: `qc.rz(0.5, 0)`**

- It rotates the qubit around the Z-axis.

**Possible questions:**
- **Q: What does the RZ gate do?**  
  A: It performs rotation around the Z-axis and changes the relative phase.
- **Q: What is the difference between RY and RZ?**  
  A: RY rotates around the Y-axis, whereas RZ rotates around the Z-axis.

### D. Creating an Entangled Pair

**Line 6: `qc.h(1)`**

- Applies a Hadamard gate to qubit 1.
- It converts the initial state \(|0\rangle\) into an equal superposition:

\[
|0\rangle \rightarrow \frac{|0\rangle+|1\rangle}{\sqrt{2}}
\]

- This prepares qubit 1 for entanglement.

**Possible questions:**
- **Q: What is a Hadamard gate?**  
  A: It is a quantum gate that creates an equal superposition when applied to \(|0\rangle\).
- **Q: What is superposition?**  
  A: It is the ability of a qubit to exist in a combination of quantum states.

**Line 7: `qc.cx(1, 2)`**

- Applies a Controlled-X gate between qubits 1 and 2.
- Qubit 1 acts as the control qubit.
- Qubit 2 acts as the target qubit.
- The target flips only when the control qubit is 1.
- Along with the previous Hadamard gate, it creates an entangled Bell pair between q1 and q2:

\[
\frac{|00\rangle+|11\rangle}{\sqrt{2}}
\]

**Possible questions:**
- **Q: What is a CX gate?**  
  A: It is a two-qubit gate that flips the target when the control is 1.
- **Q: What is a Bell pair?**  
  A: It is a maximally entangled two-qubit state.
- **Q: Which qubits are entangled here?**  
  A: Qubits 1 and 2.

**Line 8: `qc.barrier()`**

- Adds a barrier to the circuit.
- It separates the entanglement preparation stage from the next stage.
- It does not change the quantum state or perform any computation.

**Possible question:**
- **Q: Does a barrier affect the quantum state?**  
  A: No. It is used to separate circuit operations and control their ordering.

### E. Bell-State Operations

**Line 9: `qc.cx(0, 1)`**

- Applies a CX gate with q0 as the control and q1 as the target.
- This operation connects the original state with the entangled pair.
- It is one of the essential operations in the teleportation protocol.

**Possible question:**
- **Q: Why is CX applied between q0 and q1?**  
  A: To perform the first part of the Bell-state measurement procedure.

**Line 10: `qc.h(0)`**

- Applies a Hadamard gate to q0.
- It transforms the original qubit's measurement basis.
- Together, the CX and Hadamard operations prepare q0 and q1 for measurement in the computational basis.

**Possible question:**
- **Q: Why is the Hadamard gate applied after CX?**  
  A: It helps convert the Bell-basis information into computational-basis measurement outcomes.

### F. Measuring the Sender's Qubits

**Line 11: `qc.measure(0, 0)`**

- Measures qubit 0.
- Stores its result in classical bit c0.
- The measurement result will be either 0 or 1.

**Line 12: `qc.measure(1, 1)`**

- Measures qubit 1.
- Stores its result in classical bit c1.
- These two measurement results determine which correction operations must be applied to q2.

**Possible questions:**
- **Q: Why are q0 and q1 measured?**  
  A: To obtain the two classical bits required for the receiver's correction operations.
- **Q: Does measurement produce a quantum or classical result?**  
  A: It produces a classical result.
- **Q: What are c0 and c1 used for?**  
  A: They control the conditional gates applied to q2.

**Line 13: `qc.barrier()`**

Separates the measurement stage from the correction stage. It does not modify the state.

### G. Applying Conditional Corrections

This is an important part of quantum teleportation.

**Line 14: `with qc.if_test((qc.clbits[1], 1)):`**

- Checks whether classical bit c1 has the value 1.
- If the condition is true, the operation inside the block is executed.
- If c1 is 0, the operation is skipped.

**Possible question:**
- **Q: What is `if_test()`?**  
  A: It is a Qiskit conditional operation that executes a quantum instruction only when a specified classical condition is satisfied.

**Line 15: `qc.x(2)`**

- Applies an X gate to q2 if c1 is 1.
- The X gate performs a bit flip:

\[
|0\rangle \rightarrow |1\rangle
\]

\[
|1\rangle \rightarrow |0\rangle
\]

- This corrects the bit-flip error associated with the first measurement result.

**Possible questions:**
- **Q: Why is the X gate applied conditionally?**  
  A: To correct the receiver's qubit according to the measurement result.
- **Q: When is X applied?**  
  A: When c1 = 1.

**Line 16: `with qc.if_test((qc.clbits[0], 1)):`**

- Checks whether c0 is equal to 1.
- If the condition is true, the next operation is executed.

**Line 17: `qc.z(2)`**

- Applies a Z gate to q2 when c0 is 1.
- The Z gate changes the phase of the \(|1\rangle\) component:

\[
|0\rangle \rightarrow |0\rangle
\]

\[
|1\rangle \rightarrow -|1\rangle
\]

- This corrects the phase-flip error.

**Possible questions:**
- **Q: Why is the Z gate applied?**  
  A: To correct the phase of the receiver's quantum state.
- **Q: When is Z applied?**  
  A: When c0 = 1.
- **Q: What is the difference between X and Z?**  
  A: X flips the computational bit, while Z changes the phase of the \(|1\rangle\) component.

**Correction table:**

| c1 | c0 | Correction applied to q2 |
|---:|---:|---|
| 0 | 0 | No correction |
| 0 | 1 | Z gate |
| 1 | 0 | X gate |
| 1 | 1 | X gate followed by Z gate |

The order matters: this code applies X first and Z second. For these two gates, the resulting state differs from applying Z then X only by a global phase, which does not affect measurement probabilities.

### H. Measuring the Receiver's Qubit

**Line 18: `qc.measure(2, 2)`**

**Important concept:** The receiver's qubit is measured only after the correction operations. This ensures that the corrections are applied before obtaining the final result.

**Possible question:**

|---|---|

### Why Are There Multiple Results?

The original quantum state is prepared using an RY rotation of 1 radian.

Its measurement probabilities are:

\[
P(0)=\cos^2(0.5)\approx 0.7702
\]

\[
P(1)=\sin^2(0.5)\approx 0.2298
\]

The RZ gate changes the phase but does not change these computational-basis measurement probabilities.

Therefore:
- Approximately 77% of the final q2 measurements should be 0.
- Approximately 23% should be 1.
- The two sender measurement bits c0 and c1 are uniformly distributed.

All eight three-bit outcomes have nonzero probabilities. Their exact frequencies depend on the simulator's sampling.

### Understanding the Final Two Statements

**`Total Measurements: 1024`**

This confirms that the circuit was executed 1024 times.

**`Unique Measurement Results: 8`**

There are three classical bits, so the maximum possible number of different bitstrings is:

\[
2^3=8
\]

Therefore, eight unique outcomes are possible.

**Important practical observation:** Measuring q2 in the computational basis shows its 0/1 probability distribution, but it does not independently prove that the complete quantum state was teleported. To verify the full state, additional measurements in different bases or a state-fidelity calculation would be needed.

## 5. COMPLETE AIM-BASED THEORY

### 5.1 What is Quantum Teleportation?

Quantum teleportation is a quantum communication technique used to transfer an unknown quantum state from one location to another.

It uses three essential resources:

1. An original quantum state.
2. An entangled pair of qubits.
3. Two classical bits communicated from the sender to the receiver.

The original quantum state is not physically transported. Instead, its quantum information is reconstructed on the receiver's qubit.

### 5.2 How Does Quantum Teleportation Work?

The process can be understood in five steps:

**Step 1: Prepare the original state**

The sender prepares a quantum state on q0 using RY and RZ gates.

**Step 2: Create an entangled pair**

The Hadamard and CX gates create an entangled pair between q1 and q2.

- q1 belongs to the sender.
- q2 belongs to the receiver.

**Step 3: Perform Bell-state measurement operations**

The sender applies CX and Hadamard gates to q0 and q1, followed by measurements.

**Step 4: Send classical information and apply corrections**

The two measurement results are used to decide whether X and Z gates should be applied to q2.

**Step 5: Obtain the teleported state**

After applying the required corrections, q2 contains the original quantum state (up to an irrelevant global phase).

### 5.3 Role of the Sender and Receiver

| Sender | Receiver |
|---|---|
| Holds the original state q0. | Holds entangled qubit q2. |
| Shares an entangled pair with the receiver. | Shares an entangled pair with the sender. |
| Performs Bell-state measurement operations. | Applies conditional correction gates. |
| Sends two classical measurement bits. | Reconstructs the original quantum state. |

### 5.4 Why Are Classical Bits Required?

The sender obtains two classical measurement bits.

These bits tell the receiver which correction operations are necessary.

| Measurement result (c1, c0) | Operation |
|---|---|
| 00 | No operation |
| 01 | Z gate |
| 10 | X gate |
| 11 | X and Z gates |

Without the measurement information and corresponding corrections, the receiver cannot reconstruct the original state using this protocol.
