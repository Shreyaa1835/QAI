## 2. CONCEPTS USED IN THIS PRACTICAL

### 2.1 Randomized Benchmarking (RB)

Randomized Benchmarking is a technique used to measure the average error rate of quantum gates.

It works by:
1. Generating random sequences of quantum gates.
2. Applying the gates to a quantum state.
3. Applying a final gate that ideally returns the qubit to its initial state.
4. Measuring how often the initial state is recovered.
5. Repeating the experiment for different sequence lengths.

**Main idea:** If the quantum gates are accurate, the qubit should return to its initial state with high probability. As the number of gates increases, noise generally causes the survival probability to decrease.

### 2.2 Noise in Quantum Computing

Noise refers to unwanted changes in a quantum state during quantum computation.

It can occur due to:
- Imperfect quantum gates.
- Environmental disturbances.
- Hardware limitations.
- Decoherence.

### 2.3 Depolarizing Noise

Depolarizing noise is a mathematical noise model that randomly changes a quantum state.

It represents errors introduced during quantum gate operations.

In this practical, depolarizing noise is applied to single-qubit and two-qubit gates.

| SX | Square-root-of-X gate; performs a half rotation around the X-axis. |

## 5. COMPLETE AIM-BASED THEORY

### 5.1 What is Randomized Benchmarking?

Randomized Benchmarking is a method used to estimate the average error rate of quantum gates.

It helps evaluate the performance and reliability of quantum computers.

Instead of testing only one gate, it applies randomly selected sequences of quantum operations and measures how well the original state is recovered.

### 5.2 Working of Randomized Benchmarking

The process works in the following steps:

**Step 1: Generate random gate sequences**

The experiment generates random Clifford gate sequences of different lengths.

**Step 2: Apply the gate sequences**

The generated quantum gates are applied to the qubit.

**Step 3: Apply the recovery operation**

A final recovery operation is applied so that the qubit ideally returns to its original state.

**Step 4: Measure the qubit**

The qubit is measured to determine whether the expected state was recovered.

**Step 5: Repeat the experiment**

The experiment is repeated using multiple random sequences and different sequence lengths.

**Step 6: Analyse the results**

The survival probabilities are analysed to estimate the average gate error.

### 5.3 What are Clifford Gates?

Clifford gates are a special group of quantum gates that transform Pauli operators into other Pauli operators under conjugation.

Examples include:

- Hadamard (H)
- Pauli-X (X)
- Pauli-Y (Y)
- Pauli-Z (Z)
- Phase gate (S)
- CX gate

They are widely used in quantum error correction and randomized benchmarking.

### 5.4 What is Depolarizing Noise?

Depolarizing noise is a noise model that represents errors introduced during quantum operations.

For a single qubit, it can be represented as:

\[
\mathcal{E}(\rho)=(1-p)\rho+
p\frac{I}{2}
\]

Where:

- \(\rho\) = Original quantum state.
- \(p\) = Depolarizing noise parameter.
- \(I\) = Identity matrix.
- \(I/2\) = Maximally mixed single-qubit state.

As the noise parameter increases, the quantum state becomes more mixed.

### 5.5 Difference Between Single-Qubit and Two-Qubit Noise

| Single-Qubit Noise | Two-Qubit Noise |
|---|---|
| Affects one qubit. | Affects two qubits. |
| Applied to single-qubit gates. | Applied to two-qubit gates. |
| Example: X and SX gates. | Example: CX gate. |
| In this program, parameter is 0.005. | In this program, parameter is 0.05. |

### 5.6 What is Error Per Clifford (EPC)?

EPC is the estimated average error associated with one Clifford operation.

It is used to evaluate quantum gate performance.

\[
EPC=\frac{1-\alpha}{2}
\]

For single-qubit standard randomized benchmarking:

- Low EPC indicates better gate performance.
- High EPC indicates poorer gate performance.

### 5.7 Why Are Different Sequence Lengths Used?

Different sequence lengths help us understand how errors accumulate as more gates are applied.

For example:

| Sequence length | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 1 | Short sequence containing 1 Clifford operation. |
| 2 | Sequence containing 2 Clifford operations. |
| 4 | Sequence containing 4 Clifford operations. |
| 8 | Sequence containing 8 Clifford operations. |
| 16 | Sequence containing 16 Clifford operations. |

Generally, longer sequences have more opportunities for errors to accumulate.

### 5.8 Why Is Randomized Benchmarking Important?

Randomized Benchmarking is useful because it:

1. Measures average quantum gate errors.
2. Evaluates quantum computer performance.
3. Helps compare different quantum devices.
4. Studies the effect of noise on quantum circuits.
5. Helps identify whether quantum gates are sufficiently reliable.
