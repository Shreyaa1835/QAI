# 8. IDENTIFYING THE POSITION OF THE EMPTY CELL

This section uses Toffoli gates to calculate trace information for the four pairs of puzzle qubits.

### `qc.ccx(0, 1, 9)`

- Applies a Toffoli gate.
- Qubits 0 and 1 are control qubits.
- Qubit 9 is the target qubit.
- The target flips only when both control qubits are 1.

### `qc.ccx(2, 3, 10)`

- Qubits 2 and 3 are the control qubits.
- Qubit 10 is the target.
- It calculates trace information for the second pair.

### `qc.ccx(4, 5, 11)`

- Qubits 4 and 5 are the control qubits.
- Qubit 11 is the target.
- It calculates trace information for the third pair.

### `qc.ccx(6, 7, 12)`

- Qubits 6 and 7 are the control qubits.
- Qubit 12 is the target.
- It calculates trace information for the fourth pair.

### Understanding the Toffoli Gate

A Toffoli gate has:

- Two control qubits.
- One target qubit.

The target flips only when both control qubits are 1.

**Truth table:**

| Control 1 | Control 2 | Target |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0 | No flip |
| 0 | 1 | No flip |
| 1 | 0 | No flip |
| 1 | 1 | Flip |

### Important observation about your initial state

The initial puzzle pairs are:

| Pair | Initial State | Both Bits 1? | Trace Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| q0, q1 | 10 | No | 0 |
| q2, q3 | 00 | No | 0 |
| q4, q5 | 11 | Yes | 1 |
| q6, q7 | 01 | No | 0 |

Therefore, after these four Toffoli operations:

- q9 = 0
- q10 = 0
- q11 = 1
- q12 = 0

**Important technical clarification:** The Toffoli gates detect pairs containing `11`, not pairs containing `00`. If the empty cell is represented by `00`, these operations do not correctly identify it. The comments describe empty-cell identification, but the gate logic does not match that description.

**Possible Viva Questions:**

**Q1. What is a Toffoli gate?**  
A three-qubit controlled-controlled-NOT gate.

**Q2. Why are four Toffoli gates used?**  
To process the four pairs of puzzle qubits separately.

**Q3. What is the purpose of trace qubits?**  
They store intermediate information about the conditions associated with each puzzle position.

**Q4. What is the difference between CNOT and Toffoli?**  
CNOT has one control and one target, whereas Toffoli has two controls and one target.

**Q5. What is the output of a Toffoli gate when both control qubits are 0?**  
The target remains unchanged.

**Q6. Does the current Toffoli logic detect the empty cell represented by 00?**  
No. It detects the pair 11 because both control qubits must be 1.

# 10. RULE 1: EMPTY CELL AT POSITION 0, 1

This section contains clockwise and counter-clockwise movement operations.

## A. Clockwise Movement

### `qc.ccx(9, 13, 8)`

- Applies a Toffoli gate.
- Qubit 9 is the first control.
- Qubit 13 is the second control.
- Qubit 8 is the target flag.
- The flag flips when both control qubits are 1.

### `qc.cswap(8, 0, 4)`

- Applies a controlled-SWAP gate.
- Qubit 8 is the control.
- Qubits 0 and 4 are the targets.
- If qubit 8 is 1, the states of qubits 0 and 4 are exchanged.
- If qubit 8 is 0, no swap occurs.

### `qc.cswap(8, 1, 5)`

- Uses qubit 8 as the control.
- Swaps qubits 1 and 5 when the control is 1.

**Understanding both CSWAP operations:**

The two operations swap:

- Pair 0: q0 and q4.
- Pair 1: q1 and q5.

Together, they exchange the two-bit values stored in positions 0 and 2.

**Possible Viva Questions:**

**Q1. What is a CSWAP gate?**  
CSWAP stands for Controlled-SWAP. It exchanges two target qubits when the control qubit is 1.

**Q2. What is another name for CSWAP?**  
Fredkin gate.

**Q3. How many qubits are required for CSWAP?**  
Three qubits: one control and two target qubits.

**Q4. What happens when the control is 0?**  
The target qubits remain unchanged.

**Q5. What happens when the control is 1?**  
The two target qubits exchange their states.

**Q6. Why are two CSWAP gates used together?**  
To exchange two complete two-bit puzzle cells.

## B. Counter-Clockwise Movement

### `qc.ccx(9, 13, 8)`

- Applies a Toffoli gate using qubits 9 and 13 as controls.
- Qubit 8 acts as the target flag.
- It conditionally changes the flag based on the two control qubits.

### `qc.x(13)`

- Applies an X gate to the path descriptor.
- Changes its value from 0 to 1 or 1 to 0.
- It is used to change the direction condition.

### `qc.ccx(9, 13, 8)`

- Applies the Toffoli gate again.
- Updates the flag according to the current control states.

### `qc.cswap(8, 0, 2)`

- Uses qubit 8 as the control.
- Swaps qubits 0 and 2 when the flag is 1.

### `qc.cswap(8, 1, 3)`

- Uses qubit 8 as the control.
- Swaps qubits 1 and 3 when the flag is 1.

Together, these operations exchange positions 0 and 1.

### `qc.ccx(9, 13, 8)`

- Applies the Toffoli gate again.
- Updates the flag.

### `qc.x(13)`

- Applies the X gate to qubit 13 again.
- Restores the original path-descriptor basis state.

**Possible Viva Questions:**

**Q1. What is counter-clockwise movement?**  
It represents movement in the opposite direction to clockwise movement.

**Q2. Why is the X gate applied to the path descriptor?**  
It toggles the path descriptor to represent the alternate direction condition.

**Q3. Why is X applied twice in this sequence?**  
Two X operations restore the original computational-basis value because X² = I.

**Q4. What is the difference between clockwise and counter-clockwise movement?**  
They represent two opposite directions of movement in the puzzle.

# 27. IMPORTANT CONCEPTS RELATED TO THE AIM

## 1. What is Artificial Intelligence?

Artificial Intelligence (AI) is a branch of computer science that focuses on creating systems capable of performing tasks that generally require human intelligence.

Examples include:

- Problem-solving.
- Decision-making.
- Planning.
- Learning.
- Reasoning.

## 2. What is a Production System?

A production system is a problem-solving approach that uses a collection of rules to determine which actions should be performed.

It generally consists of three major components:

**1. Rule Base**

Contains the rules used to solve the problem.

Example:

IF the empty cell is at position 0, THEN move the appropriate adjacent tile.

**2. Working Memory**

Stores the current state of the problem.

Example:

The current arrangement of tiles in the puzzle.

**3. Control Strategy**

Determines which rule should be selected and executed.

Example:

Selecting clockwise or counter-clockwise movement.

**Importance:**

The production-system approach helps represent the puzzle as a collection of conditions and actions.

## 3. What is Tarrataca's Quantum Production System?

Tarrataca's Quantum Production System is an approach to representing production-system operations using quantum computational concepts.

It uses quantum states and gates to represent conditions, control operations and alternative computational paths.

In this practical, the intended idea is to represent the puzzle state and its movement rules using qubits and controlled quantum operations.

**Important:** Your code is a quantum-circuit-based representation inspired by this approach. It does not, by itself, establish a complete implementation of a general quantum production system.

## 4. What is the 3-Puzzle Problem?

The 3-Puzzle is a simplified sliding-tile puzzle.

It contains:

- Three numbered tiles.
- One empty cell.
- A 2 × 2 arrangement.

The objective is to move the tiles until the required goal configuration is obtained.

A tile can move into the empty position if it is adjacent to the empty cell.

**Example configuration:**

| 1 | 2 |
|---|---|
| 3 | Empty |

A legal move exchanges the empty cell with one adjacent tile.

## 5. What is a Puzzle Configuration?

A puzzle configuration is the current arrangement of all tiles and the empty cell.

For example:

| 1 | Empty |
|---|---|
| 2 | 3 |

A different arrangement represents a different state of the puzzle.

## 6. What is State Space?

State space is the collection of all possible states of a problem.

For a 2 × 2 puzzle containing four distinct elements:

4! = 24 possible arrangements.

However, for the standard sliding 2 × 2 puzzle, only 12 configurations are reachable from a given starting state through legal moves.

## 7. What is a Production Rule?

A production rule is a condition-action statement.

It generally follows:

IF condition THEN action.

Example:

IF the empty cell is at position 0 and movement is allowed, THEN swap the empty cell with an adjacent tile.

Production rules are used to determine legal movements.

## 8. What is State Preparation?

State preparation is the process of initializing qubits into a required state before applying quantum operations.

In your practical, X gates are used to initialize selected puzzle qubits to 1.

## 9. What is a Hadamard Gate?

The Hadamard gate creates superposition.

Mathematical representation:

H|0⟩ = (|0⟩ + |1⟩)/√2

It gives equal probability to measuring 0 or 1.

In your practical, it is applied to the path descriptor qubit.

## 10. What is a Toffoli Gate?

A Toffoli gate is a three-qubit quantum gate.

It contains:

- Two control qubits.
- One target qubit.

The target flips only when both control qubits are 1.

It is used in your program to calculate trace information and update the flag.

## 11. What is a Controlled-SWAP Gate?

A controlled-SWAP gate is also known as the Fredkin gate.

It swaps two target qubits only when the control qubit is 1.

In your practical, two CSWAP operations are used to exchange two complete puzzle positions.

## 12. What is a Flag Qubit?

A flag qubit is used to store a condition or control value.

In your practical, q8 is intended to determine whether a movement operation should take place.

## 13. What is a Trace Qubit?

A trace qubit stores intermediate information about a condition or computational path.

Your practical uses four trace qubits to represent the four possible empty-cell positions.

## 14. What is a Path Descriptor?

A path descriptor stores information about an alternative computational path.

Your program uses q13 to represent the intended movement direction.

## 15. What is Quantum Superposition?

Superposition is a fundamental quantum principle in which a qubit can exist in a combination of states.

For example:

|ψ⟩ = (|0⟩ + |1⟩)/√2

It allows quantum circuits to represent multiple computational possibilities in a single quantum state.

## 16. What is Quantum Entanglement?

Entanglement is a quantum phenomenon in which two or more qubits share a joint state and exhibit correlations.

It can be created using gates such as CNOT when the qubits are appropriately initialized.

## 17. What is a Barrier?

A barrier is an instruction that separates different sections of a quantum circuit.

It does not change the quantum state.

## 18. What is Quantum Measurement?

Measurement converts quantum information into classical information.

Each measured qubit produces either 0 or 1.

## 19. What is AerSimulator?

AerSimulator is a Qiskit Aer backend used to simulate quantum circuits on a classical computer.

## 20. What are Shots?

Shots represent the number of times a quantum circuit is executed to collect measurement outcomes.

In your practical:

Shots = 1024.

Therefore, 1,024 measurement samples are collected.

# 30. IMPORTANT TECHNICAL OBSERVATIONS ABOUT YOUR CODE

These are particularly important because an external examiner may ask you to explain whether the implementation actually follows the intended 3-Puzzle rules.

### 1. Empty-cell identification needs correction

Your comments indicate that the empty cell is represented by `00`.

However, the Toffoli gates activate when both input qubits are `1`.

Therefore, the current trace logic detects `11`, not `00`.

To detect `00`, the control logic must be designed differently, for example by applying X gates to the two inputs before the Toffoli operation and restoring them afterward.

### 2. The flag is toggled, not simply set

The Toffoli operations use q8 as a target.

A Toffoli gate flips its target when both controls are 1. It does not simply assign the flag a value of 1.

Therefore, repeated Toffoli operations can toggle the flag back and forth.

### 3. The path descriptor does not independently select two clean movement branches

The Hadamard gate puts q13 into superposition.

However, the subsequent sequence of Toffoli and X gates must be designed carefully to ensure that clockwise and counter-clockwise movements are controlled by mutually exclusive conditions.

The current sequence does not implement a straightforward conditional selection of the two directions.

### 4. The circuit does not check whether a movement is legal

A standard 3-Puzzle solver must check whether the selected tile is adjacent to the empty cell.

Your circuit uses predefined swap operations, but it does not explicitly validate all legal movement conditions or check whether the goal state has been reached.

### 5. The output is not necessarily a solution

The program prints measured configurations.

It does not calculate the shortest path, compare configurations with a goal state or verify that the generated configurations are valid puzzle solutions.

**If the external asks, "Does your code solve the 3-Puzzle completely?"**

A technically correct answer is:

"My implementation represents puzzle configurations using qubits and uses Toffoli and controlled-SWAP gates to model conditional movement operations. It also uses a path descriptor to represent alternative movement directions. However, the current version demonstrates the circuit representation rather than a complete validated puzzle-solving algorithm. Explicit empty-cell detection, legal-move validation and goal-state checking would be needed for a complete implementation."
