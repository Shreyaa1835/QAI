# 4. APPLYING THE RY ROTATION GATE

### `qc.ry(1.0, 0)`

- Applies an RY rotation gate to qubit 0.
- RY is a single-qubit quantum rotation gate.
- It rotates the quantum state around the Y-axis of the Bloch sphere.
- The first argument, 1.0, represents the rotation angle in radians.
- The second argument, 0, represents the qubit index.

**Mathematical representation:**

The RY gate is represented by:

RY(θ) =

| cos(θ/2)  -sin(θ/2) |  
| sin(θ/2)   cos(θ/2) |

Here:

θ = 1.0 radian.

The initial state of qubit 0 is |0⟩.

After applying RY(1.0):

|ψ⟩ = cos(0.5)|0⟩ + sin(0.5)|1⟩

Approximately:

|ψ⟩ = 0.8776|0⟩ + 0.4794|1⟩

Therefore:

- Probability of measuring 0 ≈ 77.02%.
- Probability of measuring 1 ≈ 22.98%.

# 24. IMPORTANT CONCEPTS RELATED TO THE AIM

## 1. What is Quantum Error Correction (QEC)?

Quantum Error Correction is a technique used to protect quantum information from errors caused by noise and unwanted interactions with the environment.

Quantum systems are sensitive to external disturbances, which can change the state of qubits.

QEC uses additional qubits and specially designed operations to detect and correct errors without directly measuring the encoded quantum information.

**Why is QEC required?**

- Quantum computers are sensitive to noise.
- Qubits can lose their quantum information.
- Quantum gates may introduce errors.
- Measurement operations may also be imperfect.

QEC improves the reliability of quantum computation.

## 2. What is a Bit-Flip Error?

A bit-flip error occurs when a qubit changes its state from 0 to 1 or from 1 to 0.

Example:

Before error:

|0⟩

After error:

|1⟩

Mathematically, a bit-flip error is represented by the Pauli-X operator.

X|0⟩ = |1⟩

X|1⟩ = |0⟩

**Example:**

If a qubit stores the information 0 and an error changes it to 1, the original information is corrupted.

The bit-flip code is designed to detect and correct this type of error.

## 3. What is the 3-Qubit Bit-Flip Code?

The 3-qubit bit-flip code is a basic quantum error-correction technique.

It uses three physical qubits to encode one logical qubit.

The encoding is:

Logical |0⟩:

|0⟩ → |000⟩

Logical |1⟩:

|1⟩ → |111⟩

For a general quantum state:

|ψ⟩ = α|0⟩ + β|1⟩

The encoded state becomes:

|ψL⟩ = α|000⟩ + β|111⟩

This encoding allows us to detect and correct a single bit-flip error.

**Example:**

Suppose the encoded state is:

|000⟩

If the second qubit flips, the state becomes:

|010⟩

The error can be detected using parity checks and corrected by flipping the second qubit again.

## 4. What is Encoding?

Encoding is the process of representing one logical qubit using multiple physical qubits.

In the 3-qubit bit-flip code:

- One logical qubit is represented using three physical qubits.
- Logical 0 becomes 000.
- Logical 1 becomes 111.

The purpose is to add redundancy so that errors can be detected and corrected.

## 5. What is Decoding?

Decoding is the process of recovering the original logical information from the encoded quantum information.

In quantum error correction, decoding involves interpreting the error syndrome and applying the appropriate correction.

## 6. What is a Logical Qubit?

A logical qubit is a qubit encoded across multiple physical qubits.

It is used to protect quantum information against physical errors.

**Difference:**

| Physical Qubit | Logical Qubit |
|---|---|
| Actual qubit in hardware. | Encoded representation of quantum information. |
| Can be affected by noise. | Uses multiple physical qubits for error protection. |
| Basic hardware unit. | Error-protected information unit. |

## 7. What is a Physical Qubit?

A physical qubit is an actual quantum system used to store and process quantum information.

Examples include:

- Superconducting circuits.
- Trapped ions.
- Photons.
- Neutral atoms.

## 8. What is a Syndrome?

A syndrome is a set of measurement results used to identify the type and location of an error.

In the 3-qubit bit-flip code, syndrome extraction uses two parity checks.

The stabilizers are:

S1 = Z₀Z₁

S2 = Z₁Z₂

Here, Z represents the Pauli-Z operator.

**Syndrome Table:**

| Error | S1 | S2 |
|---|---|---|
| No error | +1 | +1 |
| Error on qubit 0 | -1 | +1 |
| Error on qubit 1 | -1 | -1 |
| Error on qubit 2 | +1 | -1 |

The syndrome identifies which qubit has experienced a bit-flip error.

**Important:** Your current code does not explicitly perform these syndrome measurements.

## 9. What is the Pauli-X Gate?

The Pauli-X gate is a single-qubit gate that flips the state of a qubit.

It is represented by:

X =

| 0  1 |  
| 1  0 |

Its operations are:

X|0⟩ = |1⟩

X|1⟩ = |0⟩

It is also called the quantum NOT gate.

## 10. What is the Pauli-Z Gate?

The Pauli-Z gate changes the phase of a qubit.

Its matrix is:

Z =

| 1  0 |  
| 0 -1 |

Its operations are:

Z|0⟩ = |0⟩

Z|1⟩ = -|1⟩

It is associated with phase-flip errors.

## 11. What is the Bit-Flip Code?

The bit-flip code is a quantum error-correction code that protects quantum information against single-qubit bit-flip errors.

It uses three physical qubits to encode one logical qubit.

It can correct one bit-flip error among the three physical qubits.

## 12. What is a Phase-Flip Error?

A phase-flip error changes the relative phase of a quantum state.

It is represented by the Pauli-Z operator.

For example:

|+⟩ = (|0⟩ + |1⟩)/√2

After a phase-flip error:

Z|+⟩ = (|0⟩ - |1⟩)/√2

The phase of the |1⟩ component changes.

## 13. What is the Difference Between Bit-Flip and Phase-Flip Errors?

| Bit-Flip Error | Phase-Flip Error |
|---|---|
| Changes 0 to 1 or 1 to 0. | Changes the relative phase of a quantum state. |
| Represented by X gate. | Represented by Z gate. |
| Affects computational-basis information. | Affects phase information. |
| Corrected using bit-flip code. | Corrected using phase-flip code. |

## 14. What is a CNOT Gate?

CNOT stands for Controlled-NOT.

It is a two-qubit gate consisting of:

- One control qubit.
- One target qubit.

The target flips only when the control is 1.

It is commonly used in quantum error-correction encoding circuits.

## 15. What is a Toffoli Gate?

A Toffoli gate is a three-qubit controlled-controlled-NOT gate.

It has:

- Two control qubits.
- One target qubit.

The target flips only when both control qubits are 1.

It is useful in reversible computation and quantum arithmetic.

## 16. What is Quantum Noise?

Quantum noise refers to unwanted disturbances that affect the state of qubits.

Sources include:

- Environmental interactions.
- Imperfect quantum gates.
- Decoherence.
- Measurement errors.
- Control errors.

Quantum error correction helps protect quantum information against such errors.

## 17. What is Decoherence?

Decoherence is the loss of quantum coherence due to interactions between a quantum system and its environment.

It can destroy superposition and entanglement.

It is one of the major challenges in building reliable quantum computers.

## 18. What is Quantum Entanglement?

Quantum entanglement is a phenomenon in which multiple qubits share a joint quantum state and exhibit correlations that cannot be described as independent states.

It is important in quantum computing, quantum communication and quantum error correction.

## 19. What is a Quantum Simulator?

A quantum simulator is a software-based system that models the behaviour of quantum circuits.

In this practical, AerSimulator is used to execute the circuit and obtain measurement results.

## 20. Applications of Quantum Error Correction

1. **Quantum Computing:** Improves the reliability of quantum computations.
2. **Quantum Communication:** Helps protect quantum information during transmission.
3. **Quantum Cryptography:** Supports reliable quantum information processing.
4. **Quantum Research:** Helps researchers study and manage quantum noise.
5. **Fault-Tolerant Quantum Computing:** Provides the foundation for reliable large-scale quantum computation.
