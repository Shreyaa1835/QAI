## 2. CONCEPTS USED IN THIS PRACTICAL

### 2.1 Quantum Fourier Transform (QFT)

Quantum Fourier Transform is a quantum algorithm that transforms a quantum state from the computational basis into the Fourier basis.

It is the quantum equivalent of the classical Discrete Fourier Transform (DFT).

It is used in:
- Shor's factoring algorithm.
- Quantum phase estimation.
- Period finding.
- Quantum signal processing.

**Main idea:** QFT transforms the amplitudes and phases of a quantum state. It helps identify periodic patterns in quantum information.

### 2.2 Hadamard Gate (H)

The Hadamard gate creates superposition.

For example:

\[
H|0\rangle=\frac{|0\rangle+|1\rangle}{\sqrt{2}}
\]

It is used to create combinations of quantum states before applying controlled phase rotations.

### 2.3 Controlled Phase Gate (CP)

The controlled phase gate applies a phase rotation to a quantum state depending on the values of two qubits.

It is represented by:

\[
CP(\theta)
\]

Where \(\theta\) is the phase rotation angle.

### 2.4 SWAP Gate

The SWAP gate exchanges the states of two qubits.

For example:

\[
|01\rangle \rightarrow |10\rangle
\]

It is used in QFT to reverse the order of the qubits at the end.

### 2.5 Measurement

Measurement converts the quantum state into classical information, producing 0 or 1 for each measured qubit.

### 2.6 Gates Used in This Practical

| Gate | Purpose |
|---|---|
| H | Creates superposition. |
| CP | Applies a controlled phase rotation. |
| SWAP | Exchanges the states of two qubits. |
| Measure | Converts quantum information into classical bits. |

## 5. COMPLETE AIM-BASED THEORY

### 5.1 What is Quantum Fourier Transform?

Quantum Fourier Transform is a quantum operation that transforms a quantum state from the computational basis into the Fourier basis.

It is the quantum equivalent of the classical Discrete Fourier Transform.

It is useful for identifying periodic patterns and phase information in quantum states.

### 5.2 Mathematical Representation of QFT

For an n-qubit system:

\[
N=2^n
\]

The Quantum Fourier Transform is defined as:

\[
QFT|x\rangle=
\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}
\sum_{y=0}^{N-1}
e^{2\pi ixy/N}|y\rangle
\]

Where:

- \(x\) = Input state.
- \(y\) = Output state.
- \(N\) = Total number of possible states.
- \(i\) = Imaginary unit.

For this practical:

\[
n=5
\]

\[
N=2^5=32
\]

Therefore, the circuit operates on a 32-dimensional quantum state space.

### 5.3 Working of Quantum Fourier Transform

The QFT circuit works in the following stages:

**Step 1: Initialize the qubits**

Five qubits are initialized to:

\[
|00000\rangle
\]

**Step 2: Apply Hadamard gates**

Hadamard gates are applied to all five qubits.

They create superposition and allow the circuit to represent multiple computational basis states.

**Step 3: Apply controlled phase gates**

Controlled phase gates introduce phase rotations between pairs of qubits.

The rotation angle depends on the distance between the qubit indices:

\[
\theta=\frac{\pi}{2^{j-i}}
\]

These phase rotations are essential for encoding the Fourier phase relationships.

**Step 4: Apply SWAP gates**

The SWAP gates reverse the order of the qubits.

This is required in the standard QFT circuit decomposition.

**Step 5: Measure the qubits**

All five qubits are measured to obtain classical output bitstrings.

### 5.4 Why Are Hadamard Gates Used?

Hadamard gates create superposition.

They transform:

\[
|0\rangle\rightarrow
\frac{|0\rangle+|1\rangle}{\sqrt{2}}
\]

In QFT, they help construct the Fourier-transformed state.

### 5.5 Why Are Controlled Phase Gates Used?

Controlled phase gates introduce phase relationships between qubits.

These phase relationships are necessary for performing the Fourier transformation.

Unlike the X gate, a controlled phase gate does not flip the computational state. It changes the phase when both qubits are in state 1.

### 5.6 Why Are SWAP Gates Required?

In the standard QFT circuit, the output qubits are naturally produced in reversed order.

SWAP gates correct this ordering.

For five qubits, two SWAP gates are required:

- q0 ↔ q4
- q1 ↔ q3

The middle qubit q2 remains unchanged.

### 5.7 Applications of QFT

Quantum Fourier Transform is used in:

1. **Shor's Algorithm:** Used for integer factorization.
2. **Quantum Phase Estimation:** Used to estimate eigenvalues and phases.
3. **Period Finding:** Helps identify periodic patterns in functions.
4. **Quantum Algorithms:** Acts as an important component in several quantum algorithms.
5. **Quantum Signal Processing:** Helps process information represented through quantum states.

### 5.8 Difference Between DFT and QFT

| DFT | QFT |
|---|---|
| Discrete Fourier Transform. | Quantum Fourier Transform. |
| Classical algorithm. | Quantum algorithm. |
| Operates on classical data. | Operates on quantum states. |
| Uses classical mathematical operations. | Uses quantum gates. |
| Produces classical frequency-domain values. | Produces a quantum state with Fourier-transformed amplitudes and phases. |
